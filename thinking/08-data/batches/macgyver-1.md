## MG01

The lid on your bulky storage box is tightly stuck, and you can't pry it open by hand. The outer edge of the lid is flush with the body of the box, leaving no room to get a grip. You have a pair of thin socks, a brittle plastic ruler, a floppy hat, a simple hairbrush with soft bristles, a stretched out rubber band, and a wooden spoon with a smooth round handle. How can you open the lid?

## MG02

You want to plant a couple of mature tropical trees in your garden, but don't have a hole digger or spade. You have a small plastic bucket with a handle, a soft bristle paintbrush, a narrow-toothed comb, a high-pressure water hose, a large soup spoon with a wooden handle, and thin gardening gloves which are prone to tearing. Given the hard, rocky terrain and the extensive root system of the trees, ordinary digging methods are ineffective. How can you manage to plant your tropical trees?

## MG03

The wind blew off the roof of your chicken coop and you need to manually cover it before the rain comes. All you have is a wooden beam, a large piece of tarp, a pitchfork, a bag of screws, a lawn chair, and a climbing rope. The tarp is full of holes. The beam is curved and difficult to balance. The climbing rope is old and weak. How would you cover the chicken coop using just the available items?

## MG04

You notice lice in your child’s hair, but don’t have any lice shampoo or treatment available. You have a hair curler, a shower cap (which is made of thin, fragile material), vinegar, a hairdryer, olive oil (that is nearly empty), and a fine-tooth comb. Unfortunately, your child is allergic to vinegar and neither the hair curler nor the hairdryer will help in this situation. How can you treat lice?

## MG05

The battery in the classroom's wall clock has corroded and you don't have a replacement. You have a stack of index cards, a small piece of aluminum foil, vinegar, a spool of thread, cotton swabs, and a spare button battery. Here though, the spare button battery is not the right size for the clock. How can you fix the clock?

## MG06

You've been commissioned to paint a high section of a 15-feet concrete wall for a colorful mural but you don't have a ladder. Available to you is a worn-out, wooden baseball bat, a 20-feet long piece of strong, cotton rope, an unopened can of soda, a blue tarpaulin sheet large enough to cover the painting area, a few old, deflated car tires, and multiple lengths of bamboo - but, these bamboo poles are quite thin and not sturdy enough to hold body weight. How do you set yourself up to reach and paint the section without compromising your safety and display the best of your artistry?

## MG07

In a physics lab, you are tasked with measuring the coefficient of friction between two surfaces. However, the force sensor you planned to use is not working and the ruler only has marks for every 10cm. The available items are a protractor, a pair of safety goggles, a nail, a pencil, some sandpaper, a set of weights, a rubber band, a stopwatch, and a ruler. How can you measure the coefficient of friction using only these items?

## MG08

You're locked on a concrete rooftop of a ten-story building and the heavy steel door to the stairwell is badly jammed. You have available a broom with a long, wooden handle, a roughly 4-feet long wooden plank with noticeable termite holes hence not strong enough to bear your weight, a sturdy metal bucket, a colored kite with a six feet wingspan, a powerful torch with a full battery, and a 15-foot long piece of nylon rope. How can you creatively use these items to safely navigate yourself to the ground?

## MG09

You're at the beach and your sun guard umbrella is broken. You have a paperback crossword puzzle book, a thin plastic poncho with numerous small tears, two sturdy wooden sticks about a foot long, a snorkel kit with long fins, a stainless steel travel mug and a large beach towel. The poncho has multiple tears, compromising its ability to provide enough cover. How can you create a makeshift shelter from the sun?

## MG10

A cloth, a bell at the top of a 20-foot pole, a rock, a bottle, a 10-foot rope, and a stool are in your possession. The stool is too heavy to be safely swung around with the given rope, and the rope is too short to reach the bell. The rock, bottle and cloth won't help in reaching the bell either. How can you ring the bell without climbing the pole?
